# nRF9160 — design note

Companion to [`hardware/nrf9160/README.md`](../../hardware/nrf9160/README.md).

## The radio choice determines battery life, via the chemistry

The most counter-intuitive result across the eight kits:

| Kit | Radio energy per TX | Battery life (hourly) |
|---|---:|---:|
| STWIN.box, BLE | 0.29 mA·s | 1.6 y |
| nRF9160, LTE-M | **95 mA·s** | **9.4 y** |
| ESP32-S3, Wi-Fi | 240 mA·s | 1.7 y |

330× the radio energy of BLE and 5.8× the battery life. The mechanism is not the
radio:

```
LTE-M pulses 200-250 mA for tens of ms
  -> within a LiSOCl2 D-cell's capability (with a bulk capacitor)
     -> LiSOCl2 self-discharge 0.08 %/month = 4 uA
        -> circuit (40 uA) dominates the cell
           -> so the design actually matters -> 9.4 years

Wi-Fi bursts 350 mA
  -> outside a LiSOCl2 cell's pulse capability
     -> forces Li-ion at 2.5 %/month = 72 uA
        -> cell dominates above an hourly interval
           -> life saturates near 2.7 years regardless of firmware

BLE sips 8 mA
  -> any chemistry works, so the shipped 480 mAh LiPo is used
     -> 3 %/month = 20 uA against a 15 uA circuit
        -> cell dominates, saturates at 1.6 years
           -> re-celling to LiSOCl2 takes it to 15.2 years
```

The lesson generalises past this project: **on a low-duty sensor, battery life is
usually a chemistry decision dressed up as a firmware problem.** Three of the
five battery kits here have a cell whose self-discharge exceeds the circuit's
average draw, and on those the firmware cannot improve battery life at all.

## PSM, and why System OFF is wrong

`pdm_board_sleep_ms` uses Zephyr's System ON idle with the modem in PSM: ~3 µA,
SRAM retained, **registration retained**.

System OFF reaches ~2 µA — and drops the LTE registration, so the next
transmission pays a full attach instead of an RRC connection:

| | Energy |
|---|---:|
| RRC connection (registered, PSM) | ~95 mA·s |
| Full LTE attach | ~1000 mA·s |

Saving 1 µA over an hour is 3.6 mA·s. Spending 905 mA·s to get it is a bad trade
by a factor of 250. System OFF becomes correct somewhere past a one-year
interval, which is not a condition-monitoring cadence.

The PSM parameters in `prj.conf` are *requests*. Operators do not always grant
them, and a network that silently gives a 1-hour TAU instead of 10.5 hours turns
a 9-year node into a 2-year node. Verifying the granted values with `AT+CEREG?`
is on the production checklist for that reason; it is not a formality.

## NB-IoT fallback is deliberately disabled

`CONFIG_LTE_NETWORK_USE_FALLBACK=n`.

NB-IoT has better coverage and roughly 10× worse latency. A modem that falls
back automatically changes the energy budget by an order of magnitude without
telling anyone, and the symptom is a fleet where some nodes mysteriously have a
quarter of the expected battery life.

If a site needs NB-IoT, that is a per-device deployment decision recorded in the
asset registry, not something the modem decides at 3 a.m.

## The two RAM optimisations, and why they belong everywhere

256 kB, minus ~25 kB Zephyr and ~40 kB modem library, leaves ~190 kB. The naive
pipeline needs ~168 kB.

**Converting the FIFO per chunk instead of staging it** removes 48 kB and costs
nothing — the int16-to-float conversion was going to happen regardless, so doing
it inside the FIFO read loop rather than afterwards simply avoids materialising
the intermediate. The other kits stage the raw block because they can afford to,
which is a weak reason.

**Tables in flash** removes 46 kB. The interesting part is the second-order
effect: it also removes ~40 ms of boot-time transcendentals, which is a one-off
cost on a kit that sleeps with SRAM retained and a *per-cycle* cost on a kit
whose sleep mode loses RAM. So the optimisation is worth most on the ESP32-S3
(deep sleep, 512 kB) even though it was forced by the nRF9160 (256 kB, SRAM
retained). It is enabled here and available everywhere.

## The sample-rate measurement

This is the only kit that reports a measured `fs_hz_actual`.

`k_uptime_get()` runs from the 32.768 kHz LFCLK — a ±20 ppm crystal on the DK.
Timing a 307 ms acquisition to 1 ms resolution gives `samples / elapsed` to
0.33 %, against the IIS3DWB's ±3 % oscillator specification.

Why it matters, concretely: at 3 % error a 106.3 Hz BPFO line is computed as if
it were at 103.1 Hz. The matcher's tolerance is ±2 % with a 1.5-bin floor, so the
fundamental still matches. By the fifth harmonic the error is 16 Hz against a
±10.6 Hz window — the harmonic falls outside and `n_matched` drops from 5 to 3 or
4. `n_matched` is one of the 63 features the detector was *trained* on, so a kit
with a 3 % rate error produces systematically different features from one with
0.3 %, on the same machine.

The STWIN.box cannot do this (nothing there times the acquisition against a
crystal; its fix needs a reference tone through the self-test actuator). The
ESP32-S3 could and does not yet. Three kits, three answers, all written down in
their own notes.

## Open problems

- **The LTE energy figures are the least certain numbers in this repository.**
  Nordic's application notes give RRC connection energy under their own
  conditions; real networks vary by operator, band, coverage and PSM
  configuration. A 2× error either way would not be surprising and would move
  9.4 years to between 5 and 19.
- **LiSOCl₂ passivation.** A cell stored for a year grows a passivation layer
  that raises its internal resistance; the first transmission after storage can
  brown out even with the bulk capacitor fitted. A depassivation pulse at
  commissioning is the standard mitigation and is on the checklist.
- `lte_transport.c` and `nvs_queue.c` are declared and not written. The former's
  batching behaviour is what makes the energy budget work.
- **GNSS is unused.** The nRF9160 has it, and "which pump is this" is a real
  commissioning problem on a site with forty identical assets. One fix per
  deployment rather than a continuous service: a GNSS fix is ~30 s at 30 mA,
  affordable once.
